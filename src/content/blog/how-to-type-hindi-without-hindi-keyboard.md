For many of us, the need to type in Hindi (Devanagari) comes up unpredictably. You might need to reply to a family WhatsApp group, search for a specific recipe on YouTube, or fill out an official document.

If you're using a work computer, a public terminal, or just don't want to mess with your operating system's language settings, installing a dedicated Hindi keyboard is not always an option.

In this guide, we'll show you how to type flawless, grammatically correct Hindi using a virtual InScript keyboard directly in your web browser.

## Why InScript over Phonetic Transliteration?

You might be used to typing phonetically — for example, typing "namaste" to get "नमस्ते". While convenient, phonetic transliteration has major drawbacks:
- It struggles with complex conjuncts (half-letters).
- It often guesses the wrong word, requiring constant corrections.
- It relies on server-side processing, which is slower and less private.

The **InScript (Indian Script) layout** is the official Indian standard for typing Devanagari. It might take a few minutes to learn, but it is vastly superior for speed, accuracy, and handling complex vocabulary.

## Getting Started

1. Open the [GalexyKeyboard Hindi Tool](/hindi-keyboard/).
2. You will see a 3D virtual keyboard displaying Devanagari characters.
3. You can either click the keys with your mouse, or tick the **"Physical keyboard"** checkbox to type using your actual computer keyboard (your QWERTY keys will be temporarily remapped to InScript).

## Understanding the InScript Layout

The genius of the InScript layout is how logically it is organised:
- **Vowels and Vowel Signs (Matras)** are grouped on the **left side** of the keyboard.
- **Consonants** are grouped on the **right side** of the keyboard.

Because most Hindi syllables consist of a consonant followed by a vowel, your hands will naturally alternate — left, right, left, right. This ergonomic design makes typing incredibly fast once you build muscle memory.

## How to Type Conjuncts (Half-Letters)

The most common question people ask is: *How do I type half-letters like the 's' in 'Namaste' (नमस्ते)?*

The secret is the **Halant (Virama)** key: `्`. In the InScript layout, you build conjuncts by silencing the inherent vowel of a consonant using the halant.

To type a conjunct, the formula is always:
**Consonant 1 + Halant + Consonant 2**

Let's look at an example: typing **क्या** (kya):
1. Type `क` (ka)
2. Type `्` (halant)
3. Type `य` (ya)
4. Type `ा` (aa matra)

The computer automatically joins `क` and `य` to form the half-letter `क्य`.

## How to Type Independent Vowels and Aspirates

If you look closely at the keys, you'll see two characters on many of them.

To access the top character (usually an independent vowel like `अ` or an aspirated consonant like `ख`), simply press and hold the **Shift** key, just as you would for a capital letter in English.

## Copy and Paste

Once you have finished typing your message in the text box:
1. Click the **Copy** button below the text area.
2. Open WhatsApp, Facebook, Gmail, or a Word document.
3. Press `Ctrl + V` (Windows) or `Cmd + V` (Mac) to paste your perfectly formatted Hindi text.

You never have to install any software or change any system settings, and your typing remains completely private in your browser.
